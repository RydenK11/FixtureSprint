Case Study

From Manual Holding to Repeatable Batch Assembly

Result: a 46% reduction in hands-on assembly time per unit — measured across three timed assemblies, before vs. after the fixture.

A commercially sold GPU support product required three bonded components to be manually aligned and held under pressure while the adhesive cured. Because the components had to be held by hand during the full cure cycle, the operator could not begin another assembly until the bond had set—which limited throughput and made alignment inconsistent from unit to unit, since "by hand" is never perfectly repeatable.

The Fixture

The custom fixture:

  • Locates and secures three different components in the correct relative position
  • Maintains that alignment under consistent clamping pressure through the full cure cycle—no hand pressure required
  • Frees the operator to begin another assembly the moment the first is loaded and clamped, instead of waiting out the cure
  • Supports repeatable production across shifts and operators, since the fixture—not the operator's hands—sets the alignment
Before: Hands-On Time 142 sec
After: Hands-On Time 77 sec
Hands-On Time Saved 65 sec/unit
Time Reduction 46%

Across three timed assemblies, average hands-on assembly time fell from 142 seconds to 77 seconds. The fixture saved 65 seconds of operator time per unit, a 46% reduction.

Measurement note: this was a founder-built engineering project used to validate the fixture-design approach—not a paid FixtureSprint client engagement. Figures reflect three timed assemblies by one operator; results on your application will depend on its specific requirements.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Can you design a fixture if we don't have CAD?

Yes. A description of the problem, along with a few photos or rough dimensions, is enough to start. CAD, drawings, or samples help but aren't required.

Can you improve or duplicate an existing fixture?

Yes. If you can share a sample, drawing, photo, or video of the existing fixture, we can evaluate modifying or rebuilding it to fix what isn't working.

What types of production tooling can you make?

Assembly and alignment fixtures, adhesive-curing fixtures, part nests and holding aids, drill and positioning guides, go/no-go gauges, masking and inspection aids, ergonomic production tools, low-volume replacement tooling, and improvements to existing fixtures.

Are your fixtures always 3D printed?

No. FDM is one manufacturing method we use because it's fast for iterating on geometry and functional testing, but it's not the only method. Many designs mix printed and machined or off-the-shelf components depending on what the application requires.

What if the application requires metal?

Metal components can be incorporated and, where needed, machined or fabricated through outside suppliers. We'll flag this during the application review if your requirement calls for it.

What information is needed to get started?

A description of the repetitive task, existing fixture, or production requirement. Photos, drawings, CAD, or a short video are helpful but optional—send what you have.

How are project pricing and timelines determined?

Every project is scoped individually based on the application and requirements. Pricing and an estimated timeline are provided in writing before any work begins.

What testing and revisions are included?

Prototypes are physically tested against the functional requirement agreed on during scoping. The number of included revisions is documented in your written scope, since it varies by project complexity.

Will we receive the CAD/design files?

File delivery is documented in your written scope and quote, since it depends on the specifics of the agreement. Ask during the initial discussion if this matters for your project.
